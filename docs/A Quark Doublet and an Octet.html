Technical note · non-supersymmetric unification

A Quark Doublet and an Octet

github/t-0 with Claude
github.com/t-0/reality

A brute-force search over additions to the Standard Model, a robustness ranking, and one candidate that we could not find in the literature. Written for someone who works on grand unification, to ask whether it is known and whether it survives.

Summary. We built a tool that takes a proposed field content, checks it for consistency, and predicts αs(MZ) from α1, α2 and unification at two loops, with nothing fitted, together with the proton lifetime. It reproduces published results, including the Bajc–Senjanović adjoint SU(5) analysis. A search over 17 million candidate extensions found that no single multiplet unifies, rediscovered the known Q + D and split-SUSY-like solutions, and ranked as most robust among two-field models a vectorlike quark doublet Q (3,2,1/6) plus a real colour octet (8,1,0), both near 30 TeV. Embedded in SU(5) (octet from 24H, Q from a vectorlike 10 + 10̄), it unifies with αs = 0.1187 and predicts a correlation: lighter new states imply a longer-lived proton.

1. Method

A model is a file listing new fields: representation, kind (Weyl, Dirac, complex or real scalar) and mass. Optional heavy fields near the unification scale have masses given as ratios to MX. For each model:

  • Consistency gates without data: all gauge anomalies, the Witten SU(2) anomaly, integer charges for colour singlets, perturbativity to 1019 GeV.
  • Running at two loops with the top Yukawa. One- and two-loop coefficients are derived from the field content (Machacek–Vaughn); supersymmetric energy ranges are detected from the content and use the superfield form. Light thresholds are sharp; heavy fields enter by one-loop matching at MX, δi = bih/(2π) · ln(Mh/MX).
  • Held-out prediction: α1 and α2 at MZ are inputs; requiring α3 to meet them at MX gives α3(MZ), found by shooting. The pull uses the experimental error and a 2% two-loop theory error.
  • Proton decay: p → e+π0 from X/Y exchange, τ ≈ MX4/(αGUT2 mp5); for supersymmetric models also p → K+ν̄ from Hisano–Kuwahara–Nagata eq. (19). Both treated as uncertain by a factor of 10.

Re-run with today’s data, the Bajc–Senjanović benchmark is excluded: with the type-III triplet above the LHC limit (≈ 880 GeV), τ(p → e+π0) ≈ 1.5×1033 yr, and only with the most forgiving conventions does it reach 1.2×1034 yr, still below Super-K.

3. Search

Every combination of up to four multiplets from a menu of 20 (vectorlike fermions, Weyl fermions in real representations, scalars; anomaly-free by construction), at masses from 1 TeV to 1012 GeV. A model is accepted if |pull| ≤ 1 on αs, the proton outlives Super-K, MGUT ≤ 1018 GeV, and it stays perturbative.

Run Candidates Accepted Main observation
≤ 4 fields, decade masses 17,178,875 15,037 No single multiplet works. Q (3,2,1/6) is in 69% of accepted models.
≤ 3 fields, half-decade masses 9,290,430 7,404 Q in 81%. Two-field models with one mass scale appear.

Known solutions come back without being asked for: vectorlike Q + D (the standard non-SUSY setup), the colour-sextet diquark (6,1,1/3) of Babu–Mohapatra type, and wino-, gluino- and higgsino-like fermions with a second Higgs doublet at 1 TeV, which is split-SUSY content.

Robustness

A best fit at one grid point says little about tuning. For each accepted model we draw each distinct mass scale log-uniformly within ±1 decade and generic SU(5) GUT fields within 0.1–10 × MX, and count the fraction of draws that still pass, treating any draw below 1 TeV as excluded. Among two-field models the most robust is Q + real octet at ~30 TeV, 29 ± 2% (400 draws). For comparison: the MSSM with SUSY SU(5) thresholds scores 18%, and split SUSY with 109 GeV scalars 2%.

4. The candidate in SU(5)

The octet is the colour-octet part of the adjoint 24H; its partner Σ3 (1,3,0) stays heavy. Q comes from a vectorlike 10 + 10̄ of fermions with masses from a bare term and a coupling to ⟨24H⟩ ∝ diag(2,2,2,−3,−3) v:

mQ = M − λv, mU = M + 4λv, mE = M − 6λv ⇒ mQ ≈ 0 forces |mU| = |mE| = 5λv

So the partners U (3,1,2/3) and E (1,1,1) are degenerate. With them, Σ3 and the colour-triplet Higgs varied over 0.1–10 × MX:

αs(MZ), central heavy masses 0.1187 (−0.3σ)
Band over heavy masses 0.105 – 0.136
MX, central 1.5×1016 GeV
Light mass within 1σ (central heavy masses) 18 – 76 TeV
τ(p → e+π0) wherever αs fits (53 heavy-mass grid) 0.6 – 5×1036 yr

Lighter Q and octet raise MX and so lengthen the proton’s life. At a few TeV, where a collider could find them, αs needs the heavy masses at the low edge of the band and τ ≈ 1037–38 yr. Near 200–300 TeV the proton comes into Hyper-K’s reach but the states are beyond any collider. The central fit, around 37 TeV, is reachable by neither.

Cost. A light Q needs M ≈ λv to about 1 part in 1011, and the light octet a comparable tuning in the 24H potential, the same kind of tuning as doublet–triplet splitting.

5. Literature status

The ingredients are known: vectorlike Q appears in the standard Q + D setups, a real colour octet (8,1,0) helps unification in SO(10) and adjoint constructions, and close relatives with a vectorlike doublet, colour-octet scalar and real triplet exist. We did not find Q + real octet as a pair. The systematic scan we checked restricts which representations it allows and would not have tested it; Giveon–Hall–Sarid (1991) and the extended-SU(5) literature were not reviewed in full. Absence from our search is not evidence of novelty.

6. Questions for an expert

  1. Is a light vectorlike Q together with the 24H octet already discussed, perhaps under another name or inside a 45H or split-multiplet study?
  2. Is there an obstruction we have missed: the octet’s decays (a Q̄ Ta Q Σa coupling allows Σ8 → gg through a Q loop), Q mixing with Standard Model quarks, cosmological relics, or vacuum stability of the 24H potential with a light octet?
  3. Does the correlation between the light mass and the proton lifetime survive realistic GUT-scale spectra, and two-loop matching at the light thresholds?
  4. Is the tuning worse than in other non-SUSY SU(5) completions that are taken seriously?

7. Limitations and reproduction

  • Sharp light thresholds; heavy fields matched at one loop. Top Yukawa only.
  • Proton lifetimes are order-of-magnitude estimates (factor 10 either way); dimension-6 decays through the colour-triplet Higgs are not included.
  • The search finds the best models within its 20-multiplet menu, not all models.
